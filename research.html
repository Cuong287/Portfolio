01 2024–2025 · Student Research Conference Paper & Technical Study Wind Loads — TCVN 2737:2023 vs ASCE 7-16 A 20-storey reinforced-concrete building · standard calculation, ETABS modelling, and base-reaction comparison
Research question

How can wind loading for a multi-storey building be calculated under TCVN 2737:2023, and what changes when the four ASCE 7-16 loading cases — including torsional effects — are considered?

Authors & supervision

Authors: Nguyen Thanh Hau, Le Van Cuong, Nguyen Ngoc Han, and Le Nguyen Khanh Trinh.
Supervisor: Dr. Tran Thanh Binh.

My contribution

Contributed to the wind-load calculation workflow, development and checking of the Excel calculation workbook, ETABS modelling and result extraction, and preparation of comparison tables and research material.

Methodology
  • Worked through the TCVN 2737:2023 procedure for standard wind pressure, height coefficient k(ze), gust-effect factor, and aerodynamic coefficients.
  • Applied the workflow to a 20-storey, 72 m building with 3.6 m storeys.
  • Used ETABS to evaluate four ASCE 7-16 wind-action cases and compared base forces and moments.
Verified findings
  • Relative to Case 1, Cases 2 and 3 reduced both horizontal base reactions by 25%; Case 4 reduced them by 44%.
  • The torsional base moment changed by −2%, +6%, and −34% in Cases 2, 3, and 4, respectively.
  • The paper recommends considering ASCE Case 3 to account for torsional action that is not represented by separate-direction loading alone.

02 2025–2026 · Student Research Conference Paper & Research Presentation Seasonal Climate Variation and Concrete Carbonation Da Nang and Ninh Thuan · monthly-versus-annual climate inputs · carbonation only
Research question

How does retaining seasonal variation in temperature and relative humidity change long-term carbonation predictions compared with using annual climate averages?

Authors & supervision

Authors: Le Viet Nghia, Le Van Cuong, Nguyen Phuoc Nguyen Bao, Mai Van Huy, and Lai Thi Bich Van.
Supervisor: Dr. Tran Thanh Binh.

My contribution

Focused on producing and interpreting the research results, including implementing the numerical workflow in MATLAB, running Monte Carlo simulations with 1,000,000 samples, processing the outputs, and preparing the result visualizations.

Methodology
  • Applied a carbonation model incorporating atmospheric CO₂ concentration, temperature, relative humidity, and uncertain material parameters.
  • Compared monthly and annual climate inputs for Da Nang and Ninh Thuan under SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, SSP3-7.0, and SSP5-8.5.
  • Used probabilistic simulation to evaluate carbonation-depth trajectories through 2100.
Verified findings
  • Monthly climate inputs consistently produced greater predicted carbonation depth than annual averages.
  • Ninh Thuan produced greater carbonation depth than Da Nang because its lower relative humidity was more favourable to carbonation.
  • At Da Nang in 2100, the monthly-versus-yearly difference was about 2–3 mm; monthly estimates were about 10 mm under SSP1-2.6 and 16.5 mm under SSP5-8.5.
  • At Ninh Thuan, monthly predictions ranged from approximately 16 mm to 23 mm across the same low-to-high scenarios.

03 2025–2026 · Student Research Conference Paper Corrosion Risk Across Vietnamese Coastal Cities Hai Phong, Da Nang, and Ninh Thuan · chloride ingress and carbonation · probabilistic corrosion initiation
Research question

How do future climate conditions change the probability of corrosion initiation from chloride ingress and carbonation in three Vietnamese coastal cities with distinct humidity regimes?

Authors & supervision

Authors: Le Van Cuong, Le Viet Nghia, Nguyen Phuoc Nguyen Bao, Mai Van Huy, and Lai Thi Bich Van.
Supervisor: Dr. Tran Thanh Binh.

My contribution

Focused on producing and interpreting the research results, including implementing the numerical workflow in MATLAB, running Monte Carlo simulations with 1,000,000 samples, processing the outputs, and preparing the result visualizations.

Methodology
  • Combined a climate-sensitive carbonation model with a chloride-ingress model based on Fick’s second law and temperature-, age-, and humidity-dependent diffusion.
  • Evaluated Hai Phong, Da Nang, and Ninh Thuan under four SSP scenarios for 2025–2075 and 2050–2100 service windows.
  • Estimated corrosion-initiation probability against a serviceability threshold of 0.10.
Verified findings
  • Chloride-induced corrosion was the dominant mechanism in all three coastal locations.
  • The 0.10 initiation-probability threshold was exceeded after about 7–8 years in Da Nang and Hai Phong and about 10 years in Ninh Thuan.
  • By the end of the assessed service windows, chloride-initiation probability was approximately 0.37–0.38 in Da Nang and 0.31–0.34 in Hai Phong and Ninh Thuan.
  • Carbonation risk was negligible in humid Da Nang, stronger in hotter and drier Ninh Thuan, and moderate in Hai Phong for the 2050–2100 window.
