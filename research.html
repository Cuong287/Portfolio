Research question

How does climate resolution change service-life predictions?

The study compares monthly and annual climate inputs in reinforced-concrete durability models, examining initiation probability under carbonation and chloride exposure across SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, SSP3-7.0, and SSP5-8.5.

Research workflow

From climate data to reliability.

A structured workflow integrates climate processing, material models, uncertainty propagation, and surrogate modelling for long-term service-life assessment.

01

Climate data

Process historical and projected CO₂, temperature, and humidity data at monthly and annual resolution.

02

Durability models

Model carbonation and chloride ingress using climate-sensitive transport and material parameters.

03

Uncertainty

Propagate material and exposure uncertainty using Monte Carlo simulation and probabilistic indicators.

04

ANN surrogate

Prepare input–output datasets for neural-network prediction of long-term durability responses.

Current observations

What the comparison is showing.

01

Monthly climate inputs generally produce greater predicted deterioration than annual averages because seasonal extremes are retained.

02

Chloride exposure governs early service-life risk in coastal conditions, while carbonation becomes increasingly important over longer periods.

03

Differences between locations depend on the interaction of temperature and humidity rather than temperature alone.
